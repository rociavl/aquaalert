The problem

By the time you feel thirsty, you're already dehydrated.

In adults over 65, the thirst signal weakens. Add a heat wave and the body silently slides into a critical state. Most current systems react after a hospital visit.

🤫

Silent dehydration

The first signs — dizziness, confusion, fatigue — are easy to dismiss as "just being tired." Family members rarely notice in time.

⏳

No early warning

Smart bottles count sips. None of them measure what's actually happening inside the body: salivary conductance, total body water.

🚑

Reactive systems

Public health responds when someone is already hospitalized. We need something that flags risk hours — not days — earlier.

The solution

Two devices. One hydration index.

A multimodal sensor system that combines salivary chemistry and whole-body water content — fused into a single 0–100 score with tiered alerts.

How it works

From sensor to alert in seconds.

A simple data pipeline. No cloud lock-in, no opaque AI — just a clear chain from physical signal to actionable warning.

1

Sensors measure

Conductance from the mouthpiece, impedance from the ankle, temperature from both.

2

Data fuses

The bottle acts as hub. BLE merges streams into a unified timestamped record.

3

Index is calculated

A multimodal algorithm outputs a 0–100 hydration score, updated every few minutes.

4

Alert is sent

Tiered notifications: gentle nudge to user, urgent ping to caregiver if risk persists.
